# Workshop: "Using AI to Draft, Not to Grade"

**A 75-minute hands-on workshop for non-technical university staff and faculty**

- **Audience:** academic & professional staff, no technical background assumed
- **Format:** 75 min (60 min core + 15 min optional practice)
- **Language:** English + Khmer key terms
- **Goal:** staff leave able to use an AI assistant safely for *drafting* work,
  and knowing the clear boundary where a *human* must stay in control.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this workshop, participants will be able to:

1. Explain in one sentence what a generative AI assistant is and is **not**.
2. State the **"draft, don't grade"** rule and give two examples of each side.
3. List **what data must never be pasted** into an AI tool.
4. Write a basic prompt using the **Role → Task → Constraints** pattern.
5. Use one ready-made prompt from the handout to complete a real task.

---

## Materials Needed

- Projector + laptop with internet
- One shared demo account (or presenter's own approved tool)
- Printed **one-page handout** (at the end of this document)
- Post-it notes (for the "what should stay human" activity)
- Optional: a second monitor so staff can follow along on their own devices

---

## The One Rule (say this early and repeat it)

> **AI drafts. A human decides.**
> Use AI to produce a *first draft* — an email, a lesson plan, a quiz idea, a
> summary. You review it, correct it, and take responsibility for what you send
> or publish. AI never assigns a grade, never makes an admission decision, and
> never speaks for the university on its own.

**Khmer:** AI គ្រាន់តែសរសេរសេចក្តីព្រាង។ មនុស្សជាអ្នកសម្រេចចិត្ត។
*(AI only writes the draft. A human makes the decision.)*

# Slide-by-Slide Facilitator Script

## Slide 1 — Welcome & Why We're Here *(5 min)*

**On slide:** workshop title + "AI drafts. A human decides."

**Say:**
- "Today is not about turning anyone into a programmer. It's about one skill:
  using AI as a *drafting assistant* so it saves you time on repetitive writing."
- "By the end, you'll leave with prompts you can use tomorrow."
- Ask: *"Raise your hand — who has already tried ChatGPT or a similar tool?"*
  (This calibrates the room.)

---

## Slide 2 — What "AI" Means Here *(5 min)*

**On slide:** three plain-language points:

1. It's a **word-prediction machine** — very good at producing fluent text.
2. It **does not know facts** — it guesses what text *looks right*.
3. It **cannot be responsible** — only a person can be.

**Say:**
- "It sounds confident even when it's wrong. That's why we check everything."
- "It is a *writing* tool, not a *judgment* tool."

**Khmer glossary (put on slide):**
| English | Khmer |
|---|---|
| draft | សេចក្តីព្រាង |
| grade / score | ពិន្ទុ / ដាក់ពិន្ទុ |
| check / review | ពិនិត្យ |
| personal data | ទិន្នន័យផ្ទាល់ខ្លួន |
| human decides | មនុស្សជាអ្នកសម្រេច |

---

## Slide 3 — The Boundary: Draft vs. Grade *(10 min)*

**On slide:** two columns.

| ✅ AI can draft (you review) | ❌ AI must NOT decide (human owns it) |
|---|---|
| Announcement emails | Assigning marks or grades |
| Lesson-plan first draft | Admission / enrollment decisions |
| Quiz-question ideas (you verify) | Disciplinary decisions |
| Summaries of long documents | Signing off official policy |
| Rephrasing your own text | Speaking for the university |
| Rubric first draft (you finalize) | Any decision about a *person* |

**Activity (5 min, post-its):**
- "Write one work task on a post-it. Stick it on the left (safe to draft) or
  right (must stay human)." Discuss 2–3 borderline cases aloud.

**Say (the key sentence):**
- "The line is simple: **does this output change a student's outcome or speak
  for the institution?** If yes, a human owns it."

---

## Slide 4 — Live Demo: Before / After *(10 min)*

**Say:** "Watch me turn a blank page into a draft in 30 seconds — then fix it."

**Do (live, projected):**
1. Show a real, boring task: "draft an email reminding students of the
   assignment deadline."
2. Type a **bad prompt**: `"write an email about deadline"` → show the vague result.
3. Type a **good prompt** (Role → Task → Constraints):

```
Act as a university course coordinator. Write a short, polite email reminding
students that Assignment 2 is due Friday at 5pm. Include the submission link
placeholder [LINK]. Keep it under 80 words, in a friendly but professional tone.
Do not invent any policy details — I will add them.
```

4. Point out the difference: specific role, clear task, length limit, and the
   instruction *not to invent facts*.
5. Edit the result live: "I'd change this line…" to model that **the human fixes
   the draft before sending.**

---

## Slide 5 — Data Protection: What Never Goes In *(10 min)*

**On slide (red box):**

> **Never paste into an AI tool:**
> - Student names, ID numbers, or contact details
> - Grades, transcripts, or assessment results
> - Personal health, financial, or disciplinary information
> - Confidential or internal university documents without approval

**Say:**
- "If it identifies a real person or is confidential, it does not go in."
- "Use only university-approved tools. If you're unsure, ask before using."
- "When summarizing, remove or replace identifying details first."
- Quick check: *"Can I paste 'the list of students who failed the exam'?"* → **No.**

---

## Slide 6 — Hands-On Practice *(15–20 min)*

**Say:** "Pick one task from the handout. Write a prompt using the
Role → Task → Constraints pattern. Try it. Then swap with a neighbor and
critique the result."

**Give three levels (handout has full text):**
- **Beginner:** draft a short announcement email.
- **Intermediate:** draft five quiz questions on a topic you teach.
- **Advanced:** summarize a long document (after removing identifying info).

**Circulate** and reinforce: *"Did it invent anything? Did you check it? Would
you send it as-is?"*

---

## Slide 7 — Prompt Library Walkthrough *(10 min)*

**Say:** "These are copy-paste starting points. The magic words are the
constraints at the end: *'do not invent,' 'keep under X words,' 'ask me
questions before answering.'*"

Walk through 3 of the prompts from the handout (see below), and show how to
change the bracketed parts.

---

## Slide 8 — Check & Discuss *(10 min)*

**Ask the room:**
1. "What did AI get *right* that surprised you?"
2. "What did it get *wrong*, or what did it invent?"
3. "What is the one thing you will *not* do with AI after today?"

**Say:** "Number 2 is the point. The tool is fast, not reliable. Your job is to
be the reliable one."

---

**On slide:**
- "AI drafts. A human decides."
- One action: *"Use ONE prompt from the handout on a real task this week."*
- Where to get help / the approved tool list / contact.

# Prompt Library (handout content)

### Pattern to remember
```
[ROLE] + [TASK] + [CONSTRAINTS]
```
Example: *"Act as X. Do Y. Keep it under Z words. Do not invent facts."*

### 1. Announcement / reminder email
```
Act as a course coordinator. Write a short, polite email reminding students that
[ASSIGNMENT] is due on [DATE/TIME]. Mention the submission link [LINK]. Keep it
under 80 words, friendly but professional. Do not invent any policy details.
```

### 2. Five quiz questions (you verify answers)
```
Act as a lecturer in [SUBJECT]. Write five multiple-choice quiz questions on
[TOPIC] for [LEVEL] students. Give four options each and mark the correct answer.
Then list the source you based each question on, so I can verify it. Do not
include any student names.
```

### 3. Lesson-plan first draft
```
Act as an experienced teacher. Draft a 50-minute lesson plan on [TOPIC] for
[LEVEL]. Include: learning objective, a 10-minute warm-up, one main activity,
and a closing check for understanding. Use plain language. I will adapt it to
my class.
```

### 4. Rephrase my own text (make it clearer)
```
Rewrite the text below in clearer, more professional English. Keep the meaning
the same. Do not add new ideas. Keep it under [N] words.

[PASTE YOUR OWN TEXT]
```

### 5. Summarize a long document (no identifying info)
```
Summarize the document below in five bullet points. Focus on the main decisions
and action items. Do not add anything not in the text.

[PASTE TEXT WITH ALL NAMES/IDS REMOVED]
```

### 6. Rubric first draft (you finalize)
```
Act as an assessment specialist. Draft a simple marking rubric for [ASSIGNMENT].
Use four levels (Excellent, Good, Developing, Needs Work) across three criteria.
Leave the final wording to me.
```

### Khmer sample prompt (for staff who prefer to prompt in Khmer)
```
ដើរតួជាអ្នកសម្របសម្រួលវគ្គសិក្សា។ សរសេរអ៊ីមែលខ្លី និងគួរសម រំលឹកនិស្សិត
ថា កិច្ចការទី២ ត្រូវដាក់នៅថ្ងៃសុក្រ ម៉ោង ៥ល្ងាច។ សរសេរតិចជាង ៨០ ពាក្យ។
កុំបន្ថែមព័ត៌មានដែលមិនពិត។
```
*(Act as a course coordinator. Write a short, polite email reminding students
Assignment 2 is due Friday 5pm. Under 80 words. Do not add false information.)*

# One-Page Handout (print this side)

**AI drafts. A human decides.**

- ✅ Use AI to **draft**: emails, lesson plans, quiz ideas, summaries, rephrasing.
- ❌ Never let AI **decide**: grades, admissions, discipline, official policy.

**Never paste in:** names, IDs, grades, contact details, or confidential documents.

**Prompt pattern:** `Role + Task + Constraints` — always add *"do not invent facts."*

**Golden habit:** Read it. Check the facts. Edit it. Then send it — *you* are the author.

**Your first task this week:** use prompt #1 to draft one real email.

## Facilitator Notes

- **Time-keeping:** if short on time, compress Slide 5 (data protection) — but
  never skip it; it's the governance element the role requires.
- **Bilingual delivery:** read each key term in English then Khmer. If the room
  is mixed, the Khmer glossary on Slide 2 keeps everyone together.
- **Follow-up:** offer a 30-min "bring your own task" session the next week;
  this is how you demonstrate *capability transfer*, not one-off training.
